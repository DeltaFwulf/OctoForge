
# Chamber Insulation

The chamber is the core of the insulation in the furnace, designed to support the crucible and heating elements. Unlike the majority of the insulation in the furnace, this subassembly must be rigid and so will be made from [insulating firebricks](https://shop.vitcas.com/insulating-fire-bricks-vitcas-grade-26.html). These come in different grades, with different maximum service temperatures; we will use grade 26 in the first design iteration.

The furnace will approximate a cylinder with its octagonal prism design - the cylinder being the best shape for thermal efficiency (without having a doubly-curved design - no thanks).

The selected crucible has a diameter of 50 mm. To give ample clearance, the chamber diameter (across flats) is set at 70 mm. The 76 mm brick depth is gives the wall depth; by using the brick in this orientation, the chamber can use only a single brick per side.

| Dimension | Length (mm) |
| --------- | ----------- |
| Depth     | 76          |
| Width     | 114         |
| Height    | 226         |

The crucible has a height of 150mm below the neck; this corresponds to the brick height minus its depth. The base, therefore, will use the depth to give an equal insulation thickness of 76 mm, and the chamber has a total height of 226 mm. If bricks come with large variance on their height, the base can be trimmed along with the height to maintain the critical 150 mm internal height dimension.

The furnace uses 4 [[Heating Element Design|heating elements]] to generate heat. These are to be placed within grooves cut into the inner walls of the chamber, spaced evenly for even heat delivery to the crucible and walls (might make things last a bit longer?). To supply current to these elements, threaded bars must pass through the bricks, aligned to the grooves, and a counterbore provided to clear the nuts clamping onto the element wires.

Lastly, the chamber must have two extra holes to accept a pair of thermocouples, which will sense chamber temperature during operation. The holes may be oversized, as rockwool will be used to seal them and retain the sensors.

# Lid Insulation

The lid will be insulated using rockwool, adhered to the lid using high temperature adhesive. This must be cut down to give clearance for all fasteners in the lid.
